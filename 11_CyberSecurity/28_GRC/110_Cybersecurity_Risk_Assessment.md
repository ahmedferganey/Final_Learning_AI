> Phase 28 — GRC

This course is designed as a self-contained module using:

```text
Context
  ↓
Objective / Requirement
  ↓
Risk / Threat / Obligation
  ↓

  ↓
```

---

## 1. Topic Title

**Cybersecurity Risk Assessment**

---

## 2. Learning Objectives

- Write clear cybersecurity risk scenarios and define assessment scope/context.
- Identify assets, threat sources/events, vulnerabilities, predisposing conditions, and controls.
- Estimate inherent, residual, and target risk.
- Use qualitative, semi-quantitative, and quantitative approaches appropriately.
- Explain likelihood, impact, SLE/ARO/ALE, distributions, and Monte Carlo awareness.
- Use NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1 concepts and relate assessments to RMF/CSF.
- Build a risk register with owners, treatment, due dates, assumptions, and evidence.
- Analyze third-party, cloud, application, OT, AI, and supply-chain risk scenarios.
- Define treatment choices, exceptions, acceptance, and review triggers.
- Communicate risk to executives without false precision.

## 4. Core Concepts Explanation

# Part 1 — Cybersecurity Risk Assessment Purpose

### Core Explanation

Cybersecurity risk assessment identifies and analyzes threat scenarios so decision-makers can prioritize treatment based on likelihood and impact.

### Diagram / Example

```text
Context
  ↓
Identify risk
  ↓
Analyze
  ↓
Evaluate
  ↓
Treat
  ↓
Monitor / communicate
```

### Why It Matters

This topic connects technical security to business, safety, operational, legal, or assurance outcomes.

### Practical Use

Apply the concept using documented architectures, read-only evidence, synthetic scenarios, policy/control matrices, or explicitly authorized lab environments.

### Common Problems

- Treating compliance as equal to security.
- Treating a risk score as objective truth.
- Assigning controls without clear owners.
- Collecting evidence that does not actually prove the control works.
- Using generic IT changes in fragile OT environments.
- Ignoring business or safety consequences.
- Keeping exceptions open indefinitely.

### Best Practice

Define the objective, owner, evidence, decision criteria, residual risk, and review cycle. For OT, coordinate every meaningful change with operations and safety stakeholders.

## 9. Common Mistakes & Best Practices

### Common Mistakes

- Copying a framework without adapting it to business context.
- Treating risk matrices as mathematically precise.
- Confusing inherent and residual risk.
- Accepting risk without an accountable owner.
- Creating policies that cannot be evidenced or enforced.
- Collecting screenshots instead of reliable evidence.
- Letting control exceptions remain permanent.
- Treating OT like ordinary office IT.
- Making production OT changes without operations and safety approval.

### Best Practices

- Start from business/process objectives.
- Define ownership.
- Use repeatable risk scenarios.
- Map controls to explicit objectives.
- Keep evidence current.
- Separate design effectiveness from operating effectiveness.
- Track residual risk.
- Time-bound exceptions.
- Review continuously.
- In OT, preserve safety and availability first.

## 10. Self-Assessment Questions (with short answers)

### Q1. What is the key lesson from **Cybersecurity Risk Assessment Purpose**?

**Short answer:** Cybersecurity risk assessment identifies and analyzes threat scenarios so decision-makers can prioritize treatment based on likelihood and impact.

### Q2. What is the key lesson from **Risk**?

**Short answer:** Risk is the effect of uncertainty on objectives and in cybersecurity is commonly expressed through threat scenarios, likelihood, impact, and controls.

### Q3. What is the key lesson from **Risk Scenario**?

**Short answer:** A risk scenario describes a plausible event connecting threat, weakness, asset/process, and business consequence.

### Q4. What is the key lesson from **Risk Statement**?

**Short answer:** A useful statement explains how a threat could exploit a condition and produce a defined impact.

### Q5. What is the key lesson from **Assessment Scope**?

**Short answer:** Define organization, process, system, data, location, technology, third party, and time horizon included.

### Q6. What is the key lesson from **Assessment Context**?

**Short answer:** Business objectives, regulatory obligations, architecture, threat environment, and risk appetite shape the assessment.

### Q7. What is the key lesson from **Stakeholder**?

**Short answer:** Include business owners, system owners, security, IT, operations, privacy, legal, vendors, and risk owners as appropriate.

### Q8. What is the key lesson from **Asset**?

**Short answer:** Assets include systems, data, identities, services, people, suppliers, facilities, reputation, and business processes.

### Q9. What is the key lesson from **Asset Owner**?

**Short answer:** Owners provide value, criticality, dependency, and impact context.

### Q10. What is the key lesson from **Asset Criticality**?

**Short answer:** Criticality should reflect mission, revenue, safety, customers, legal obligations, and recovery.

### Q11. What is the key lesson from **Threat Source**?

**Short answer:** Threat sources may be malicious actors, insiders, accidents, suppliers, environmental events, or technology failures.

### Q12. What is the key lesson from **Threat Event**?

**Short answer:** A threat event is the specific action or occurrence that could cause harm.

### Q13. What is the key lesson from **Vulnerability**?

**Short answer:** A vulnerability is a weakness that can be exploited or trigger adverse outcomes.

### Q14. What is the key lesson from **Predisposing Condition**?

**Short answer:** A predisposing condition increases likelihood or impact even when it is not a discrete vulnerability.

### Q15. What is the key lesson from **Exposure**?

**Short answer:** Exposure reflects how reachable or accessible an asset/weakness is to a threat.

### Q16. What is the key lesson from **Control**?

**Short answer:** Existing controls affect likelihood, impact, detection, response, and recovery.

### Q17. What is the key lesson from **Control Effectiveness**?

**Short answer:** Do not assume a documented control is effective without evidence.

### Q18. What is the key lesson from **Inherent Risk**?

**Short answer:** Inherent risk estimates exposure before considering the effect of current controls.

### Q19. What is the key lesson from **Residual Risk**?

**Short answer:** Residual risk remains after considering existing controls.

### Q20. What is the key lesson from **Target Risk**?

**Short answer:** Target risk represents the desired risk level after planned treatment.

### Q21. What is the key lesson from **Risk Appetite**?

**Short answer:** Appetite sets the broad amount/type of risk leadership is willing to accept.

### Q22. What is the key lesson from **Risk Tolerance**?

**Short answer:** Tolerance provides practical thresholds for variation around objectives.

### Q23. What is the key lesson from **Risk Acceptance Criteria**?

**Short answer:** Define who can accept which level/type of residual risk and for how long.

### Q24. What is the key lesson from **Likelihood**?

**Short answer:** Likelihood estimates the chance a scenario will occur over a defined period.

### Q25. What is the key lesson from **Threat Likelihood**?

**Short answer:** Threat likelihood considers capability, intent, activity, targeting, and opportunity.

### Q26. What is the key lesson from **Vulnerability Exploitability**?

**Short answer:** Exploitability considers preconditions, exposure, complexity, privileges, user interaction, and control bypass.

### Q27. What is the key lesson from **Impact**?

**Short answer:** Impact estimates consequences if the scenario occurs.

### Q28. What is the key lesson from **Confidentiality Impact**?

**Short answer:** Consider unauthorized disclosure and downstream business/privacy effects.

### Q29. What is the key lesson from **Integrity Impact**?

**Short answer:** Consider unauthorized modification of data, systems, transactions, or decisions.

### Q30. What is the key lesson from **Availability Impact**?

**Short answer:** Consider downtime, degraded service, recovery time, and operational dependencies.

### Q31. What is the key lesson from **Financial Impact**?

**Short answer:** Include revenue loss, recovery cost, contractual penalties, fraud, and business interruption.

### Q32. What is the key lesson from **Legal / Regulatory Impact**?

**Short answer:** Consider legal duties and penalties with appropriate legal/compliance input.

### Q33. What is the key lesson from **Reputational Impact**?

**Short answer:** Consider customer trust, partner confidence, public impact, and strategic consequences.

### Q34. What is the key lesson from **Safety Impact**?

**Short answer:** OT, healthcare, transportation, and cyber-physical scenarios may require explicit safety impact analysis.

### Q35. What is the key lesson from **Operational Impact**?

**Short answer:** Consider production, service delivery, quality, logistics, and staff productivity.

### Q36. What is the key lesson from **Qualitative Assessment**?

**Short answer:** Qualitative methods use ordered categories such as low/medium/high or 1–5 scales.

### Q37. What is the key lesson from **Risk Matrix**?

**Short answer:** A matrix combines likelihood and impact categories according to organization-defined thresholds.

### Q38. What is the key lesson from **Matrix Limitation**?

**Short answer:** Risk matrices simplify communication but can hide uncertainty and create false precision.

### Q39. What is the key lesson from **Semi-Quantitative Assessment**?

**Short answer:** Semi-quantitative approaches use numeric scores while still relying on ordinal judgment.

### Q40. What is the key lesson from **Quantitative Assessment**?

**Short answer:** Quantitative analysis estimates loss or frequency using monetary or probabilistic models.

### Q41. What is the key lesson from **SLE Awareness**?

**Short answer:** Single Loss Expectancy estimates loss from one event under simplified quantitative models.

### Q42. What is the key lesson from **Exposure Factor**?

**Short answer:** Exposure Factor estimates the percentage of asset value lost in one event in simplified models.

### Q43. What is the key lesson from **ARO Awareness**?

**Short answer:** Annualized Rate of Occurrence estimates expected event frequency per year.

### Q44. What is the key lesson from **ALE Awareness**?

**Short answer:** Annualized Loss Expectancy equals SLE multiplied by ARO in simplified calculations.

### Q45. What is the key lesson from **Quantitative Limitation**?

**Short answer:** Poor input data can make numeric results look more precise than the underlying evidence supports.

### Q46. What is the key lesson from **Probability Distribution**?

**Short answer:** Ranges and distributions can represent uncertainty more honestly than single-point estimates.

### Q47. What is the key lesson from **Monte Carlo Awareness**?

**Short answer:** Simulation can propagate uncertain inputs into a distribution of possible outcomes.

### Q48. What is the key lesson from **FAIR Awareness**?

**Short answer:** FAIR is a quantitative information-risk analysis framework based on frequency and magnitude concepts.

### Q49. What is the key lesson from **NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1 Awareness**?

**Short answer:** NIST SP 800-30 Rev.

### Q50. What is the key lesson from **NIST RMF Relationship**?

**Short answer:** Risk assessments inform control selection, authorization, monitoring, and broader enterprise risk decisions.

### Q51. What is the key lesson from **NIST CSF Relationship**?

**Short answer:** CSF Profiles and Govern/Identify outcomes can support understanding and prioritizing cybersecurity risk.

### Q52. What is the key lesson from **Threat Intelligence Input**?

**Short answer:** CTI can improve scenario relevance and likelihood judgments.

### Q53. What is the key lesson from **Vulnerability Input**?

**Short answer:** Scanner findings, penetration tests, configuration reviews, and advisories provide technical inputs.

### Q54. What is the key lesson from **Incident Input**?

**Short answer:** Past incidents reveal real attack paths, control failures, and impact.

### Q55. What is the key lesson from **Audit Input**?

### Q60. What is the key lesson from **Evidence**?

### Q62. What is the key lesson from **Risk Register**?

### Q66. What is the key lesson from **Mitigate**?

### Q67. What is the key lesson from **Avoid**?

### Q68. What is the key lesson from **Transfer**?

### Q69. What is the key lesson from **Accept**?

### Q70. What is the key lesson from **Compensating Control**?

### Q76. What is the key lesson from **Systemic Risk**?

### Q77. What is the key lesson from **Risk Interdependency**?

### Q78. What is the key lesson from **Third-Party Risk**?

### Q89. What is the key lesson from **Trigger-Based Review**?

### Q90. What is the key lesson from **Continuous Risk Monitoring**?

### Q91. What is the key lesson from **Risk Communication**?
