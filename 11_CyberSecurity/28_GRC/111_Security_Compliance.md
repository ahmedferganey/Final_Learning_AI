## 1. Topic Title

**Security Compliance**

---

## 2. Learning Objectives

- Build an obligation register and determine scope/applicability.
- Map external requirements to internal controls and owners.
- Collect reliable audit evidence and distinguish design from operating effectiveness.
- Run gap assessments, audits, findings, corrective actions, and retesting.
- Explain ISO/IEC 27001:2022 ISMS concepts including scope, risk, SoA, audit, management review, and improvement.
- Understand SOC 2, PCI DSS, privacy/GDPR, NIST/CIS assurance concepts at a foundational level.
- Govern third-party, cloud, SaaS, privacy, retention, and evidence requirements.
- Design continuous compliance and continuous control monitoring.
- Manage exceptions, waivers, certifications, attestations, and audit readiness.
- Build a unified compliance program that avoids last-minute audit behavior.

## 4. Core Concepts Explanation

# Part 1 — Security Compliance Purpose

### Core Explanation

Security compliance demonstrates that applicable cybersecurity and information-security obligations are identified, implemented, evidenced, assessed, and maintained.

### Diagram / Example

```text
Law / Regulation / Contract / Standard
          ↓
Obligation
          ↓
Control Requirement
          ↓
Owner
          ↓
Evidence
          ↓
Assessment
```

### Why It Matters

This topic connects technical security to business, safety, operational, legal, or assurance outcomes.

### Practical Use

Apply the concept using documented architectures, read-only evidence, synthetic scenarios, policy/control matrices, or explicitly authorized lab environments.

### Common Problems

- Treating compliance as equal to security.
- Treating a risk score as objective truth.
- Assigning controls without clear owners.
- Collecting evidence that does not actually prove the control works.
- Using generic IT changes in fragile OT environments.
- Ignoring business or safety consequences.
- Keeping exceptions open indefinitely.

### Best Practice

Define the objective, owner, evidence, decision criteria, residual risk, and review cycle. For OT, coordinate every meaningful change with operations and safety stakeholders.

## 9. Common Mistakes & Best Practices

### Common Mistakes

- Copying a framework without adapting it to business context.
- Treating risk matrices as mathematically precise.
- Confusing inherent and residual risk.
- Accepting risk without an accountable owner.
- Creating policies that cannot be evidenced or enforced.
- Collecting screenshots instead of reliable evidence.
- Letting control exceptions remain permanent.
- Treating OT like ordinary office IT.
- Making production OT changes without operations and safety approval.

### Best Practices

- Start from business/process objectives.
- Define ownership.
- Use repeatable risk scenarios.
- Map controls to explicit objectives.
- Keep evidence current.
- Separate design effectiveness from operating effectiveness.
- Track residual risk.
- Time-bound exceptions.
- Review continuously.
- In OT, preserve safety and availability first.

## 10. Self-Assessment Questions (with short answers)

### Q1. What is the key lesson from **Security Compliance Purpose**?

**Short answer:** Security compliance demonstrates that applicable cybersecurity and information-security obligations are identified, implemented, evidenced, assessed, and maintained.

### Q2. What is the key lesson from **Compliance vs Security**?

**Short answer:** Compliance is conformance to defined requirements; strong security also requires risk-based protection beyond minimum requirements.

### Q3. What is the key lesson from **Obligation**?

**Short answer:** Obligations may arise from laws, regulations, contracts, standards, customer commitments, or internal policy.

### Q4. What is the key lesson from **Obligation Register**?

**Short answer:** Maintain an inventory of applicable obligations, owners, scope, evidence, and review dates.

### Q5. What is the key lesson from **Applicability**?

**Short answer:** Determine whether a requirement applies based on geography, data, industry, service, contract, or certification scope.

### Q6. What is the key lesson from **Scope**?

**Short answer:** Compliance scope defines systems, processes, entities, locations, data, and third parties covered.

### Q7. What is the key lesson from **Control Mapping**?

**Short answer:** Map external requirements to internal controls without assuming one control satisfies every nuance.

### Q8. What is the key lesson from **Common Control Framework**?

**Short answer:** A common internal control library can reduce duplication across multiple frameworks.

### Q9. What is the key lesson from **Requirement Owner**?

**Short answer:** Each obligation needs an accountable owner who understands business context.

### Q10. What is the key lesson from **Control Owner**?

**Short answer:** Control owners operate or oversee the safeguards used to meet requirements.

### Q11. What is the key lesson from **Evidence Owner**?

**Short answer:** Evidence ownership ensures required artifacts are collected consistently.

### Q12. What is the key lesson from **Evidence**?

**Short answer:** Evidence demonstrates that a control exists and operated during the required period.

### Q13. What is the key lesson from **Evidence Relevance**?

**Short answer:** Evidence must directly support the control assertion being tested.

### Q14. What is the key lesson from **Evidence Completeness**?

**Short answer:** Evidence should cover the required population, period, system, and control frequency.

### Q15. What is the key lesson from **Evidence Reliability**?

**Short answer:** System-generated, independently verifiable evidence may provide stronger assurance than self-attestation alone.

### Q16. What is the key lesson from **Evidence Retention**?

**Short answer:** Retain compliance evidence according to legal, contractual, audit, and privacy requirements.

### Q17. What is the key lesson from **Evidence Repository**?

**Short answer:** Use structured access-controlled storage for policies, reports, exports, samples, screenshots, and approvals.

### Q18. What is the key lesson from **Screenshot Limitation**?

**Short answer:** Screenshots can be useful but often provide weaker evidence than configuration exports, logs, or system reports.

### Q19. What is the key lesson from **Population**?

### Q20. What is the key lesson from **Sampling**?

**Short answer:** Auditors may test a sample when evaluating recurring controls, depending on methodology.

**Short answer:** Design testing asks whether the control is appropriately designed to meet the objective.

### Q22. What is the key lesson from **Test of Operating Effectiveness**?

### Q23. What is the key lesson from **Inquiry**?

**Short answer:** Inquiry gathers explanations from control owners but normally needs corroborating evidence.

### Q24. What is the key lesson from **Inspection**?

**Short answer:** Inspection reviews documents, configurations, records, or artifacts.

### Q25. What is the key lesson from **Observation**?

**Short answer:** Observation watches a control being performed.

### Q26. What is the key lesson from **Reperformance**?

**Short answer:** Reperformance independently executes or verifies a control activity.

### Q27. What is the key lesson from **Internal Audit**?

**Short answer:** Internal audit provides independent assurance over governance, risks, controls, and compliance.

### Q28. What is the key lesson from **External Audit**?

**Short answer:** Independent external parties may perform certifications, attestations, customer audits, or regulatory examinations.

### Q29. What is the key lesson from **Audit Plan**?

### Q30. What is the key lesson from **Audit Request List**?

### Q69. What is the key lesson from **Retention Compliance**?
