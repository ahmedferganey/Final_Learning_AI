# Documentation, collaboration and product thinking

> **Priority:** Core throughout · **Study window:** 1–12 · **Approximate focused time:** Integrated (overlaps with weekly plan)

## Why it is universal

Correct technical work requires shared understanding of what is needed, what changed and how success is measured.

## Detailed table of contents / concepts to cover

- [ ] Concise README, setup/run/test instructions and examples
- [ ] Issue and PR descriptions with context and acceptance criteria
- [ ] Design docs and architecture decision records
- [ ] Assumptions, open questions, risks and limitations
- [ ] Review etiquette and evidence-backed technical disagreement
- [ ] Product success metrics and user-oriented feedback

## How to learn it

1. Write a five-sentence problem statement before every medium feature.
2. Keep all documentation accurate as code evolves.
3. Ask another person or AI to attempt installation from your README and record where instructions fail.

## Required exercise

Produce a complete capstone handover: problem, assumptions, acceptance criteria, architecture, runbook, test evidence and known limitations.

## Independent exit criteria

- [ ] Can explain the project to a new developer in five minutes.
- [ ] Can produce reproducible instructions.
- [ ] Can distinguish an observed result from an opinion or assumption.

## AI collaboration exercise

Ask an AI assistant for an alternative approach, at least three failure scenarios and a code/design review. Validate the suggestions independently, make one targeted improvement and describe what you accepted or rejected and why. Never treat the AI answer itself as evidence of correctness.
